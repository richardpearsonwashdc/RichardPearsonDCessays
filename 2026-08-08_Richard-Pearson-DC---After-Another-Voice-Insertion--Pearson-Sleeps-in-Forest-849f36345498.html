The Pinging Map

On August 5, Pearson walked along Rock Creek Park through Georgetown toward the DC-Maryland line. He had few clicks once he was riverside shortly before reaching Georgetown. It was an indication of weakening signals.

“I was on a trail in the woods. The clicking had stopped. I thought maybe one or two passersby triggered it.”

Pearson did have a voice insertion. This white woman, 20’s, passed him. He noticed that she was in all black. Other than that, he was focused on walking.

“Special Agent.”

Pearson and the woman were about 20 feet apart then Pearson heard a foreign voice. “Special Agent.” Pearson has been here before — an attempt to convince him that he is somehow in or was in the US military or intelligence. He described the man at the Anacostia Park last year with the shirt, “You can always re-enlist.” The man found Pearson in a secluded area. Pearson says that this is part of some “psychological whatever”. “This is torture. Plain and simple.”

Pearson was alone on the trail except for the woman. He looked over his shoulder back at her. He called her a name under his breath many times, as he saw more people pass on bikes.

Clicks began picking up when the trail got closer to the highway. “I was nearing the state line when it picked up and could clearly hear when the woods cleared on the right and the DC District line sign was visible.” The device is triggered by Bluetooth-enabled lamp posts and cars, the disappearing forest lost the buffer between these and the device in his skull. The clicking continued for some time after that until the forest between the highway and trail became dense again. Pearson eventually stopped for the night, settling on a bench. The canopy of the forest was too thick for the moonlight to show the path. Rather than risk it, he stopped.

Pearson was mostly restless but getting some intermittent sleep.

“Serial Killer”

Pearson jumped up when he heard a voice, “Serial killer on trail.” He had no idea of the time. “Where the hell did that come from?” Pearson, panicking, looked up and down the trail. As he suspected, no one. He looked upward at the sky. To his left slightly obstructed by the treetops were two aircraft. “They appeared vertically rectangular with a light at the top. It think it’s because of the night that I didn’t make out the entire plane. There were definitely two of them. They also were not commercial aircraft. First, they never appear this close to each other. These were especially close. Second, these were hovering. I cannot tell you what happened and why.”

Pearson took precaution and slept the remainder of the night behind the bench. He was still restless in the morning. He opted for rest deeper in the woods. It worked. It was also restorative.

No Clicks

Still, nothing. “I thought that I was in a dead zone, like a blackout area. I saw all of the nice houses. Rich people exempted?”

Pearson took a free bus to Wheaton, then Silver Spring, then the metro back home. Nothing. On August 6, he was back in the city. On August 7, still no clicks.
