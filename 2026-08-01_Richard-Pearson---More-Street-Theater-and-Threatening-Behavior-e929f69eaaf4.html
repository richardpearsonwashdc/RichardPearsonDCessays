1. “You’re Not Being Tortured”

Pearson is taking a break from writing at the Martin Luther King, Jr Library in downtown Washington, DC. He crosses the street in front of a blue car, which he assumes is like any other car. He is avoiding people on account of his horrific torture in the nation’s capital. Just as he steps on the sidewalk, he hears it, “You’re not being tortured.” Just like the countless voice insertions that he has experienced, he has honed his skills in differentiating his own voice from a foreign voice. He looks back at the car — a Black woman is sitting in the car. It dawns on Pearson. “It’s an unmarked police car! She did that!” The woman seems to be in some sort of uniform, but Pearson doesn’t study her much to describe it. He is focused on getting away from the car. He only sees blue clothing with what appears to be some white top. Pearson remarks on the woman’s state of mind, “These people have been using these mind control tools for so long that parts of their brain have shut down — the parts that govern moral judgment and restraint. The dopamine hits, combined with the institutional consent, are changing the very structure of their brains. This lady thought that it was perfectly acceptable what she did. Cognitive liberty is an inviolable right that has been consistently upheld by the US Supreme Court. These people think that they are gods and above the law. They can’t even understand why it’s torture.”

Pearson has noted an increased use of vehicles to send voices into his head — likely ultrasonic sound waves that is not audible until two waves of different wavelengths demodulate inside the skull.
